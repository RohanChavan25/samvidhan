# Module 1 · How the Constitution was made ✅

> The early Session 1–2 chat explainers cited secondary sites (Wikipedia, PolSci Institute) and flagged unchecked Hindi. They were later re-verified and superseded by the image cards (P01–P19, C01–C03). Treat the cards as the canonical text.


## C01 · How the Constitution was made (1)
*संविधान कैसे बना (Samvidhan kaise bana)*

- **16 May 1946** — The British Cabinet Mission announces its plan for a Constituent Assembly (संविधान सभा).
- **July 1946** — Elections: provincial assemblies elect the members (indirect election).
- **9 Dec 1946** — First meeting, Constitution Hall, New Delhi. Muslim League boycotts. Sachchidananda Sinha chairs as oldest member; on 11 Dec, Rajendra Prasad is elected permanent Chairman.
- **13 Dec 1946** — Nehru moves the Objectives Resolution (उद्देश्य प्रस्ताव). Adopted 22 Jan 1947.
- **15 Aug 1947** — Independence and Partition. By Dec 1947: 299 members (229 provinces, 70 princely states).
- **29 Aug 1947** — Drafting Committee (प्रारूप समिति) of 7 appointed, chaired by Dr B.R. Ambedkar.

*Source: Cabinet Mission Plan, 16 May 1946; 1946 election reports; Lok Sabha (sansad.in); Constituent Assembly Debates 9 Dec 1946, 11 Dec 1946, 22 Jan 1947, 29 Aug 1947; constitutionofindia.net*

## C02 · How the Constitution was made (2)
*संविधान कैसे बना (Samvidhan kaise bana)*

- **Oct 1947** — B.N. Rau, Constitutional Adviser, completes a draft. The Drafting Committee examines it from 27 Oct 1947.
- **21 Feb 1948** — Draft Constitution published for public comment.
- **4 Nov 1948** — Ambedkar introduces the draft. Debated article by article: 15 Nov 1948 to 17 Oct 1949.
- **26 Nov 1949** — Adopted and enacted (अंगीकृत और अधिनियमित). A few articles start the same day (Article 394).
- **24 Jan 1950** — Members sign 3 copies. Rajendra Prasad elected first President; Jana Gana Mana declared National Anthem.
- **26 Jan 1950** — Comes into force (लागू). India becomes a Republic. Date honours Purna Swaraj Day, 26 Jan 1930.

**Total time: 2 years, 11 months and 17 days · 11 sessions · 165 days of sittings**

*Source: constitutionofindia.net (Stages of Constitution Making; Purna Swaraj); Constituent Assembly Debates 24 Jan 1950; Article 394; Lok Sabha (sansad.in)*

## C03 · Adopt, enact, come into force
*अंगीकृत · अधिनियमित · लागू (Angeekrit · Adhiniyamit · Lagoo)*

- **Adopt · अंगीकृत करना · 26 Nov 1949** — The Assembly formally accepts the final text. Like in school: the committee approves the final rulebook.
- **Enact · अधिनियमित करना · 26 Nov 1949** — The accepted text is made law. Like in school: the principal stamps it as official.
- **Come into force · लागू होना · 26 Jan 1950** — The law starts working (Article 394 calls it “the commencement”). Like in school: printed in November, used from the new term.

**Quiz trap: the Preamble says 26 Nov 1949, not 26 Jan 1950.**

*Source: Preamble and Article 394, Constitution of India (official text, 1 May 2024); Hindi: Hindi Wikipedia, LiveLaw Hindi*

## Session plans (teacher-facing)

### Session 1 — Desh ki Rulebook (60 min)
- 0–8 min: 'Class rules' game. No rules for a week: what happens? Which 3 rules first? Who makes them? What if the monitor breaks them? Point: the Constitution decides who makes rules, limits rulers, guarantees citizens' rights.
- 8–15 min: no-marks check-up (Republic Day, Constitution Day, Drafting Committee chair, Preamble, one Fundamental Right, voting age, Parliament, President vs PM).
- 15–35 min: the story (9 Dec 1946 → 26 Nov 1949 → 26 Jan 1950; why 26 Jan: Purna Swaraj 1930; written by hand).
- 35–45 min: clip from Samvidhaan (Shyam Benegal), Episode 1, Hindi. Teacher previews and picks the segment.
- 45–55 min: 5-question recall quiz on today's story.
- 55–60 min: each student says one new thing. Homework: ask a family member '26 January ka matlab kya hai aapke liye?'

### Session 2 — The Preamble (60 min, tail end recovered)
- 40–50 min: 'Kaunsa shabd?' game — 10 everyday situations, students name the Preamble word (e.g. a rich and poor man both get one vote → Political Justice).
- 50–56 min: rapid fire — Objectives Resolution (moved 13 Dec 1946, accepted 22 Jan 1947); 42nd Amendment 1976 is the only change; Socialist, Secular, Integrity added; Berubari 1960; Kesavananda Bharati 1973; date in the Preamble is 26 Nov 1949.
- 56–60 min: each student explains one word in Hindi. Homework: one real example each of Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity from school, basti or news.
- Watch for: students who can recite the words but cannot explain Democratic vs Republic.

# Module 2 · The Preamble ✅ (19 cards)


## P01 · The Preamble: India’s promise — उद्देशिका: भारत का वादा
- The Preamble: India's promise
- उद्देशिका: भारत का वादा
- (Uddeshika: Bharat ka vaada)
- The opening paragraph of the Constitution. In a few lines it answers four questions.
- WHO made it?
- We, the People of India
- हम, भारत के लोग
- WHAT is India?
- Sovereign · Socialist · Secular · Democratic · Republic
- WHAT does India promise?
- Justice · Liberty · Equality · Fraternity
- न्याय · स्वतंत्रता · समता · बन्धुता
- WHEN was it adopted?
- 26 November 1949
- Like in school: The first page of your school diary says what the school stands for. The rest of the diary is the rules.
- Source: Constitution of India (official text, 1 May 2024)

## P02 · The full text — उद्देशिका का पूरा पाठ
- The full text
- उद्देशिका का पूरा पाठ
- (Uddeshika ka poora paath)
- WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC and to secure to all its citizens:
- JUSTICE, social, economic and political;
- LIBERTY of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship;
- EQUALITY of status and of opportunity;
- and to promote among them all
- FRATERNITY assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation;
- IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION.
- Highlighted words were added by the 42nd Amendment (in force 3 January 1977).
- Source: Constitution of India (official text, 1 May 2024); 42nd Amendment Act, 1976

## P03 · We, the People of India — हम, भारत के लोग
- WHO MADE IT
- We, the People of India
- हम, भारत के लोग
- (Hum, Bharat ke log)
- Meaning
- The Constitution’s authority comes from the people of India, not from a king or a foreign ruler.
- Example
- Every citizen aged 18 or above has the right to vote, unless disqualified by law.
- Like in school
- The class rules are written by the whole class, not handed down by one person.
- Promise kept in
- Article 326: elections on the basis of adult suffrage
- Source: Constitution of India (official text, 1 May 2024), Article 326

## P04 · Sovereign — संपूर्ण प्रभुत्व-संपन्न
- WHAT INDIA IS
- Sovereign
- संपूर्ण प्रभुत्व-संपन्न
- (Sampoorn prabhutva-sampann)
- Meaning
- India makes its own decisions. No outside power controls its laws or government.
- Example
- India’s Parliament, not any foreign government, decides India’s laws.
- Like in school
- Your school makes its own rules; another school cannot make rules for it.
- Promise kept in
- The whole Constitution: all power comes from it
- Source: Constitution of India (official text, 1 May 2024)

## P05 · Socialist — समाजवादी
- WHAT INDIA IS
- ADDED IN 1976
- Socialist
- समाजवादी
- (Samajwadi)
- Meaning
- The State works for the economic and social upliftment of all people. In 2024 the Supreme Court said this does not stop private business.
- Example
- Free and compulsory education for every child aged 6 to 14 (Article 21A).
- Like in school
- The school makes sure every child has books, not only those who can pay.
- Promise kept in
- Article 38: the State shall promote the welfare of the people
- Source: Constitution of India; 42nd Amendment Act, 1976; Dr Balram Singh v. Union of India, 25 Nov 2024

## P06 · Secular — पंथनिरपेक्ष
- WHAT INDIA IS
- ADDED IN 1976
- Secular
- पंथनिरपेक्ष
- (Panthnirpeksh)
- Meaning
- The State has no religion of its own. It “neither supports any religion nor penalizes the profession and practice of any faith” (Supreme Court, 2024).
- Example
- Every person may follow, practise and spread their religion, subject to public order, morality and health.
- Like in school
- No student is treated better or worse because of their religion.
- Promise kept in
- Article 25 (freedom of religion) and Article 15 (no discrimination on grounds of religion)
- Source: Constitution of India, Articles 15 and 25; Dr Balram Singh v. Union of India, 25 Nov 2024

## P07 · Democratic — लोकतंत्रात्मक
- WHAT INDIA IS
- Democratic
- लोकतंत्रात्मक
- (Loktantratmak)
- Meaning
- The people choose their government through elections and can change it at the next election.
- Example
- Voting for an MLA, MP or corporator.
- Like in school
- The class monitor is chosen by the students’ votes.
- Promise kept in
- Article 326: adult suffrage
- Source: Constitution of India (official text, 1 May 2024), Article 326

## P08 · Republic — गणराज्य
- WHAT INDIA IS
- Republic
- गणराज्य
- (Ganrajya)
- Meaning
- The head of the country is elected, not born into the job. There is no king or queen.
- Example
- The President is elected by the elected members of Parliament and of the State Legislative Assemblies.
- Like in school
- The head girl or head boy is chosen by election, not because a parent held the post.
- Promise kept in
- Article 54: election of the President
- Source: Constitution of India (official text, 1 May 2024), Article 54

## P09 · Justice — न्याय
- WHAT INDIA PROMISES
- Justice
- न्याय
- (Nyay)
- Meaning
- Fairness of three kinds: social (no one treated as lower by birth), economic (a fair chance to earn and live) and political (an equal say in elections).
- In Hindi
- सामाजिक, आर्थिक और राजनीतिक न्याय
- Example
- Untouchability is abolished, and practising it in any form is forbidden.
- Like in school
- Every child gets the same seat, the same water tap and the same chance to answer.
- Promise kept in
- Articles 15 and 17 (social) · Article 38 (economic) · Article 326 (political)
- Source: Constitution of India, Articles 15, 17, 38, 326; Hindi Preamble text

## P10 · Liberty — स्वतंत्रता
- WHAT INDIA PROMISES
- Liberty
- स्वतंत्रता
- (Swatantrata)
- Thought
- Expression
- Belief
- Faith
- Worship
- Meaning
- Freedom of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship.
- In Hindi
- विचार, अभिव्यक्ति, विश्वास, धर्म और उपासना की स्वतंत्रता
- Example
- Writing to a newspaper about a bad road. The law can still set reasonable limits, for example for public order.
- Like in school
- You may give your opinion in class, but you may not insult or threaten others.
- Promise kept in
- Article 19(1)(a), with limits under Article 19(2) · Article 25 (religion)
- Source: Constitution of India, Articles 19 and 25; Hindi Preamble text

## P11 · Equality — समता
- WHAT INDIA PROMISES
- Equality
- समता
- (Samata)
- Equality of status
- Equality of opportunity
- Meaning
- Equality of status (प्रतिष्ठा) and of opportunity (अवसर).
- Why it matters
- The same law applies to a minister and a vegetable seller, and everyone gets a fair chance.
- Example
- Government jobs are open to all citizens, and the State cannot give people titles (only military or academic distinctions are allowed).
- Like in school
- Same exam, same rules and same marking for every student.
- Promise kept in
- Article 14 · Article 16 · Article 18
- Source: Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16, 18; Hindi Preamble text

## P12 · Fraternity — बन्धुता
- WHAT INDIA PROMISES
- Fraternity
- बन्धुता
- (Bandhuta)
- Meaning
- Brotherhood and sisterhood among all Indians, assuring the dignity of every person and the unity and integrity of the nation.
- In Hindi
- व्यक्ति की गरिमा और राष्ट्र की एकता और अखण्डता
- Did you know?
- The word “integrity” was added in 1976. “Fraternity” was added by the Drafting Committee; it was not in the Objectives Resolution.
- Like in school
- Classmates help each other instead of forming groups that leave others out.
- Promise kept in
- Article 51A(e): a Fundamental Duty to promote harmony and common brotherhood
- Source: Constitution of India, Article 51A; 42nd Amendment Act, 1976; Ambedkar’s note with the Draft Constitution, 1948

## P13 · Adopt, enact and give to ourselves — अंगीकृत, अधिनियमित और आत्मार्पित
- WHEN
- Adopt, enact and give to ourselves
- अंगीकृत, अधिनियमित और आत्मार्पित
- (Angeekrit, adhiniyamit aur aatmarpit)
- 26 November 1949
- Adopt
- Accept the final text.
- Enact
- Make it law.
- Give to ourselves
- The people own it. This is why 26 November is Constitution Day.
- Like in school
- The class signs its own rulebook, so everyone owns it.
- Quiz trap: the date inside the Preamble is 26 Nov 1949, not 26 Jan 1950.
- Source: Constitution of India, Preamble (official text, 1 May 2024); Hindi Preamble text

## P14 · Why the promises go together — स्वतंत्रता · समता · बन्धुता
- Why the promises go together
- स्वतंत्रता · समता · बन्धुता
- (Swatantrata · Samata · Bandhuta)
- Liberty
- स्वतंत्रता
- Equality
- समता
- Fraternity
- बन्धुता
- Separate any one, and democracy fails
- These principles of liberty, equality and fraternity form a union of trinity in the sense that to divorce one from the other is to defeat the very purpose of democracy.
- — Dr B.R. Ambedkar, Constituent Assembly, 25 November 1949
- Freedom without equality lets the strong push the weak around.
- Equality without freedom means nobody can choose.
- Without fraternity, people will not respect each other's freedom or equality.
- Source: Dr B.R. Ambedkar, Constituent Assembly, 25 Nov 1949

- Supreme Court
- Change to the text

## The big four

### Dr Rajendra Prasad · डॉ. राजेन्द्र प्रसाद
*President of the Constituent Assembly · संविधान सभा के अध्यक्ष*
- Born 3 December 1884 in Siwan district, Bihar. Represented Bihar.
- Elected permanent Chairman of the Assembly on 11 December 1946 (the post was later called President).
- Chaired four committees: Rules of Procedure, Steering, Finance and Staff, and the Ad hoc Committee on the National Flag.
- On 24 January 1950 the Assembly elected him the first President of India. He is the only President re-elected for a second term.
- *Quiz hook:* Number clue: "four committees". Connection: President of the Assembly AND first President of India.
- *Sources:* M1, M3, M4, M12

### Dr B.R. Ambedkar · डॉ. भीमराव अम्बेडकर
*Chairman, Drafting Committee · प्रारूप समिति के अध्यक्ष*
- Born 14 April 1891 in Mhow Army Cantonment, Central Provinces.
- Studied at Elphinstone College, Bombay, then Columbia University and the University of London.
- Represented Bengal and then Bombay in the Assembly. Chaired the Drafting Committee, which sat for 141 days from 30 August 1947.
- First Law Minister of independent India (1947). On 9 December 1948 he called the remedies article (now Article 32) "the very soul of the Constitution and the very heart of it".
- *Quiz hook:* Mumbai link: Elphinstone College. Number clue: 141 days. Quote clue: "soul ... heart" = Article 32.
- *Sources:* M4, M8, M10

### Jawaharlal Nehru · जवाहरलाल नेहरू
*Moved the Objectives Resolution · उद्देश्य प्रस्ताव के प्रस्तावक*
- Represented the United Provinces.
- Moved the Objectives Resolution on 13 December 1946. The Preamble is based on it.
- Chaired the Union Powers Committee and the Union Constitution Committee.
- Gave the "tryst with destiny" speech in the Constituent Assembly at the midnight session of 14–15 August 1947.
- *Quiz hook:* Connection: Objectives Resolution + Union Powers + Union Constitution. Place clue: "tryst with destiny" was spoken inside the Assembly.
- *Sources:* M4, M6, M9

### Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel · सरदार वल्लभभाई पटेल
*Chairman, Advisory Committee and Provincial Constitution Committee · सलाहकार समिति के अध्यक्ष*
- Represented Bombay.
- Chaired the Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Minorities and Tribal and Excluded Areas.
- Chaired the Provincial Constitution Committee (elected chairman on 5 May 1947).
- Led the negotiations that brought the princely states into the Indian Union, earning the name "Iron Man of India". Seconded Rajendra Prasad’s nomination as President on 24 January 1950.
- *Quiz hook:* Connection: rights + minorities + provinces. Mumbai link: represented Bombay.
- *Sources:* M4, M6, M12

## Story posters (30, in 9 chapters)

### Chapter 1 Why did India need its own Constitution? भारत को अपना संविधान क्यों चाहिए था?

**1. Rules made far away** — भारत को अपना संविधान क्यों चाहिए था?

For a long time, the laws that governed India were made under British rule. The Government of India Act 1935, for example, was passed by the British Parliament in London. Indians wanted to decide their own rules.
- Before 1947, India was ruled by Britain.
- The Government of India Act 1935 was passed by the British Parliament in London.
- Many Indians said: our rules should be written by Indians.
- *Mithu says: Our country, our rulebook!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net*

**2. The dream of Purna Swaraj** — भारत को अपना संविधान क्यों चाहिए था?

On 26 January 1930, Indians publicly declared that their goal was Purna Swaraj, complete independence. In June 1934 the Congress officially demanded a Constituent Assembly: an elected body that would write India’s own constitution.
- 26 January 1930: Purna Swaraj (complete independence) was declared publicly.
- June 1934: the Congress officially demanded a Constituent Assembly.
- A Constituent Assembly is a group of elected people who write a constitution.
- *Mithu says: Constituent Assembly = the Constitution-writing team!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net*

**3. The plan, then the elections** — भारत को अपना संविधान क्यों चाहिए था?

On 16 May 1946 the British Cabinet Mission announced a plan for the Constituent Assembly. In July 1946 the members were chosen. Ordinary voters did not vote directly; members of the provincial assemblies chose them. There were 389 seats, and the princely states could get up to 93 of them.
- 16 May 1946: the British Cabinet Mission announced its plan.
- July 1946: elections were held.
- Members of the provincial assemblies voted to choose the members (an indirect election).
- 389 seats in all; the princely states could get up to 93 of them.
- *Mithu says: Plan first, then elections!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net; en.wikipedia.org; sansad.in*

### Chapter 2 Meet the team टीम से मिलो

**4. Dr Rajendra Prasad** — डॉ. राजेन्द्र प्रसाद

Rajendra Prasad was a successful lawyer from Bihar. In 1917 he joined Gandhi in Champaran, where indigo farmers were being treated unfairly. In 1920 he gave up law to join the independence movement. He was jailed during the Salt Satyagraha (1931) and the Quit India movement (1942).
- Born 3 December 1884, Siwan, Bihar.
- A lawyer who joined Gandhi in the Champaran Satyagraha of 1917 for indigo farmers.
- Gave up his law career in 1920 to join the freedom movement.
- President of the Congress three times; jailed in 1931 and 1942.
- *Mithu says: From lawyer to freedom fighter!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net; en.wikipedia.org*

**5. Jawaharlal Nehru** — जवाहरलाल नेहरू

Jawaharlal Nehru trained as a lawyer. In December 1929 he presided over the Lahore session of the Congress, which made complete independence the goal. He spent close to nine years in prison and became independent India’s first Prime Minister.
- Born 14 November 1889, Allahabad.
- A lawyer (advocate) who joined the freedom movement.
- Presided over the Lahore Congress of December 1929, which chose Purna Swaraj as the goal.

**6. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** — सरदार वल्लभभाई पटेल

Vallabhbhai Patel was a barrister. In 1928 he led the farmers of Bardoli in a peaceful protest against higher taxes. He presided over the Congress at Karachi in 1931. After independence he led the talks that brought the princely states into India, and became known as the "Iron Man of India".
- Born 31 October 1875.
- A barrister who led the Bardoli Satyagraha of 1928, a farmers’ protest against higher taxes.

**7. Dr B.R. Ambedkar** — डॉ. भीमराव अम्बेडकर

Bhimrao Ambedkar faced untouchability as a child. He went on to study in Bombay, New York (Columbia University) and London. On 20 March 1927 at Mahad, he led Dalits to a public water tank, the Chavdar tank, and they drank its water. His fight for equality shaped the Constitution.
- Born 14 April 1891, Mhow. As a child he faced untouchability.
- Studied at Elphinstone College, Bombay, then Columbia University and the University of London.

**8. Sarojini Naidu** — सरोजिनी नायडू

Sarojini Naidu was a famous poet, called "the Nightingale of India". In 1925 she became the first Indian woman to preside over the Congress, at Kanpur. She was arrested five times for taking part in the freedom movement.
- Born 13 February 1879, Hyderabad.

### Chapter 5 Dividing the work: committees काम का बँटवारा: समितियाँ

**15. What is a committee?** — काम का बँटवारा: समितियाँ

Writing a constitution is a huge job. So the Assembly split the work among committees. Each committee studied one subject and sent a report; the full Assembly discussed these reports, and they were used to prepare the draft.
- The Assembly had too much work for one big group.
- So it set up committees: small groups, each with one job.
- Each committee studied its subject and sent a report.
- The whole Assembly then discussed the reports.
- *Mithu says: Many small teams make big work easy!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net; sansad.in*

**16. Union Powers Committee** — काम का बँटवारा: समितियाँ

This committee, led by Nehru, made a list of subjects the central government would handle, such as defence, relations with other countries (foreign affairs), and communications.
- Chairman: Jawaharlal Nehru.
- Its job: list the subjects the central (Union) government would control.

This committee, also led by Nehru, planned the central government: an elected President at the top, and a Parliament with two houses, the Council of States and the House of the People.
- Chairman: Jawaharlal Nehru.
- Its job: plan how the central government would be built.
- It proposed an elected President as head.

This committee, chaired by Patel, worked on the rights every citizen would have, and on protections for minorities and tribal areas. Smaller sub-committees handled each part.
- Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Minorities and Tribal and Excluded Areas. Formed 24 January 1947.

- *Mithu says: Prasad wore four hats!* · *Source: sansad.in; constitutionofindia.net*

**21. The Drafting Committee** — काम का बँटवारा: समितियाँ

The most famous committee was the Drafting Committee, led by Ambedkar. Its job was to check the draft prepared by B.N. Rau and turn it into a revised Draft Constitution. It met for 141 days.
- Appointed 29 August 1947. Chairman: Dr B.R. Ambedkar.
- Its job: check the draft prepared by the Constitutional Adviser, B.N. Rau, and prepare a revised draft.
- Members: Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar, N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar, K.M. Munshi, Mohammad Saadulla, B.L. Mitter, D.P. Khaitan.
- It sat for 141 days.
- *Mithu says: 7 members, 141 days!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net*

### Chapter 6 Freedom at midnight आधी रात की आज़ादी

**22. The midnight session** — आधी रात की आज़ादी

India became free at midnight, and the Constituent Assembly was meeting at that very moment. Songs, Nehru’s famous speech, a pledge and the presentation of the flag made it a night to remember.
- 14 August 1947, 11 p.m.: session begins; Sucheta Kripalani sings Vande Mataram.
- Nehru: “Long years ago we made a tryst with destiny…”
- At midnight: members take the pledge.
- Hansa Mehta presents the National Flag “on behalf of the women of India”.
- The end: Sare Jahan Se Achcha and Jana Gana Mana.
- *Mithu says: Freedom arrived inside the Assembly!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net*

**23. After freedom** — आधी रात की आज़ादी

After Partition, the Assembly had 299 members. It now had two jobs: writing the Constitution, and making ordinary laws for the new country.
- After Partition, the Assembly became smaller.
- By December 1947: 299 members (229 from provinces, 70 from princely states).
- The Assembly also worked as India’s law-making body; G.V. Mavalankar presided when it met as a legislature.
- *Mithu says: Two jobs: writing the Constitution and making laws!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net*

### Chapter 7 Writing the Constitution संविधान लिखना

**24. Heroes behind the scenes** — संविधान लिखना

Two experts worked behind the scenes: B.N. Rau prepared the first draft, and S.N. Mukherjee, the Chief Draftsman, turned difficult ideas into clear legal language. Ambedkar publicly thanked them both.
- B.N. Rau, the Constitutional Adviser, finished a draft by October 1947.
- S.N. Mukherjee was the Chief Draftsman.
- Ambedkar gave both of them credit in his speech of 25 November 1949.
- *Mithu says: Quizmasters love backstage heroes!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net; jajharkhand.in*

**25. The draft goes public** — संविधान लिखना

The draft was shared with the public in February 1948. From November 1948 to October 1949, members went through it article by article, arguing and improving it.
- 21 February 1948: the Draft Constitution was published for people to comment on.
- 4 November 1948: Ambedkar introduced it in the Assembly.
- 15 November 1948 to 17 October 1949: members discussed it article by article.
- *Mithu says: Everyone could read the draft!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net*

**26. Great debates** — संविधान लिखना

The debates were lively. Members disagreed, voted and changed the draft. Some lines from these debates are famous quiz questions today.
- 5 November 1948: T.T. Krishnamachari said the burden of drafting fell on Ambedkar.
- 15 November 1948: K.T. Shah proposed calling India “Secular, Federal, Socialist”. Rejected.
- 9 December 1948: Ambedkar called Article 32 “the very soul of the Constitution and the very heart of it”.
- 17 October 1949: H.V. Kamath’s “In the name of God” proposal was defeated, 41 for and 68 against.
- *Mithu says: Who said it? A favourite quiz question!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net; main.sci.gov.in*

### Chapter 8 The finishing line मंज़िल

**27. Adopted!** — मंज़िल

After almost three years, on 26 November 1949, the Assembly adopted the Constitution. That is why we celebrate Constitution Day on 26 November.
- 26 November 1949: the Constitution was adopted and enacted.
- A few articles started that same day (Article 394).
- Today, 26 November is Constitution Day.
- *Mithu says: Almost 3 years of hard work!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net; sansad.in*

**28. Signed, and it begins** — मंज़िल

On 24 January 1950 the members signed the Constitution. Two days later, on 26 January 1950, it came into force, and India became a Republic.
- 24 January 1950: members signed three copies: handwritten English, printed English and handwritten Hindi.
- The handwritten English copy: calligraphy by Prem Behari Narain Raizada, art by Nandalal Bose and artists of Santiniketan.
- The same day: Rajendra Prasad was elected India’s first President, and Jana Gana Mana was declared the National Anthem.
- 26 January 1950: the Constitution came into force.
- *Mithu says: Signed on 24 January, started on 26 January!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net*

### Chapter 9 What happened next आगे क्या हुआ

**29. Later “firsts”** — आगे क्या हुआ

Many members went on to hold important "firsts" in independent India and in the world.
- First President: Dr Rajendra Prasad.
- First Law Minister: Dr B.R. Ambedkar.
- First Health Minister: Rajkumari Amrit Kaur.
- First Speaker of the Lok Sabha: G.V. Mavalankar.
- First woman Chief Minister: Sucheta Kripalani (Uttar Pradesh, 1963).
- First woman President of the UN General Assembly: Vijaya Lakshmi Pandit (1953).
- Judge of the International Court of Justice: B.N. Rau (1952 to 1953).
- *Mithu says: “Later became…” is a classic clue!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net*

**30. Think like a quizmaster** — आगे क्या हुआ

Quiz questions about people usually give clues. Learn each person as a set of clues: a place, a date, a job, a famous line and a later role.
- Who am I? Clues go from hardest to easiest.
- Connect: find the hidden link.
- Odd one out: find the group of three.
- Numbers: 389, 299, 15, 7, 141.
- Who said it? Match the line to the person.
- *Mithu says: Think, link, answer!* · *Source: sansad.in; constitutionofindia.net*

# Module 4 · The 10 Makers and the Founding Mothers ✅


### Chapter 1 Two books for our quiz क्विज़ के लिए दो किताबें

**1. Meet the 10 Makers** — 10 निर्माता

The quiz organisers recommend Karthik Venkatesh’s book "10 Makers of the Indian Constitution". It tells the stories of ten women and men who helped shape the Constitution. Some are famous; others are less known but very important. In this module we meet all ten, one by one.
- Book: 10 Makers of the Indian Constitution, by Karthik Venkatesh (2025).
- Dr B.R. Ambedkar · Jawaharlal Nehru · Jaipal Singh Munda · Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar · K.M. Munshi
- Hansa Mehta · Dakshayani Velayudhan · G. Durgabai · Hasrat Mohani · J.J.M. Nichols Roy
- *Mithu says: 10 people, 10 stories!* · *Source: penguin.co.in*

### Chapter 2 The 10 Makers 10 निर्माता

**2. Dr B.R. Ambedkar** — डॉ. भीमराव अम्बेडकर

Ambedkar led the seven-member Drafting Committee, which checked and revised the draft of the Constitution. He introduced the draft in the Assembly on 4 November 1948. He called Article 32, the right to go to the Supreme Court to protect your rights, the heart and soul of the Constitution.
- Chairman of the Drafting Committee (appointed 29 August 1947).
- The committee sat for 141 days.
- Called Article 32 “the very soul of the Constitution and the very heart of it” (9 December 1948).
- *Mithu says: Chairman, 141 days, heart and soul!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net*

**3. Jawaharlal Nehru** — जवाहरलाल नेहरू

Nehru gave the Assembly its aims. His Objectives Resolution of 13 December 1946 said what kind of country India would be. The Preamble is based on it. He also led two committees that planned the central government.
- Moved the Objectives Resolution: 13 December 1946.
- It was adopted on 22 January 1947; the Preamble is based on it.
- Chaired the Union Powers Committee and the Union Constitution Committee.
- *Mithu says: Nehru wrote the aims first!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net*

**4. Jaipal Singh Munda** — जयपाल सिंह मुंडा

Jaipal Singh Munda captained India to its first Olympic gold in hockey in 1928. In the Constituent Assembly he spoke for the Adivasis, India’s tribal peoples. On 19 December 1946 he said, "I am proud to be a Jungli", and spoke "on behalf of more than 30 millions of the Adibasis", who had been "neglected for the last 6,000 years".
- Born 3 January 1903, Khunti, Ranchi district, in a Munda tribal family.
- Studied at St John’s College, Oxford.
- Captain of India’s hockey team at the 1928 Amsterdam Olympics: India’s first Olympic gold.
- President of the Adivasi Mahasabha from 1939.
- “I am proud to be a Jungli” (19 December 1946).
- *Mithu says: Olympic captain and Adivasi voice!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net*

**5. Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar** — अल्लादि कृष्णस्वामी अय्यर

Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar was Advocate-General of Madras, the top government lawyer of the province, from 1929 to 1944. As a member of the Drafting Committee, he helped shape the rules on citizenship, fundamental rights and emergencies.
- Born 14 May 1883, Pudur village.
- Advocate-General of Madras, 1929 to 1944.
- Member of the Drafting Committee.
- Spoke on citizenship, fundamental rights and emergency powers.
- *Mithu says: A top lawyer on the drafting team!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net*

**6. K.M. Munshi** — के.एम. मुंशी

K.M. Munshi was a Bombay lawyer and a well-known novelist. He sat on 16 committees and sub-committees. The compromise on India’s official language, approved on 14 September 1949, is called the Munshi–Ayyangar formula. Later he came up with Van Mahotsav, the tree-planting festival.
- Born 30 December 1887, Bharuch, Gujarat. Practised law in Bombay.
- A writer of historical novels. Founded the Bharatiya Vidya Bhavan in 1938.
- Member of 16 committees and sub-committees, including the Drafting Committee.
- Munshi–Ayyangar formula on official language: approved 14 September 1949.
- *Mithu says: Lawyer, novelist, tree-planter!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net; en.wikipedia.org*

**7. Hansa Mehta** — हंसा मेहता

Hansa Mehta fought for equal rights for women. In 1946 she led the All India Women’s Conference and drafted a charter of women’s rights. At the United Nations she made sure the Universal Declaration of Human Rights said "All human beings", not "All men".
- Born 3 July 1897, Surat. Elected from Bombay.
- President of the All India Women’s Conference, 1946.
- Drafted the Indian Women’s Charter of Rights and Duties.
- At the UN, she changed “All men are born free and equal” to “All human beings are born free and equal”.
- *Mithu says: Human beings, not just men!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net*

**8. Dakshayani Velayudhan** — दाक्षायणी वेलायुधन

Dakshayani Velayudhan grew up on a small island in Kerala and belonged to the Pulaya community, a Scheduled Caste. She was the only Dalit woman in the Assembly. She argued that the remedy for untouchability was "sustained state propaganda and not through punishment".
- Born 15 July 1912, Mulavukad island, Kerala.
- The only Dalit woman member of the Constituent Assembly. Elected from Madras, aged about 34.
- Opposed separate electorates for Scheduled Castes.
- Wanted her life story titled “The Sea has no Caste”.
- *Mithu says: The sea has no caste!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net*

**9. G. Durgabai** — जी. दुर्गाबाई

Durgabai joined the freedom struggle as a child. She was jailed twice, completed her M.A. while in prison, and later studied law. In the Assembly she spoke on language, independent judges and human trafficking. Later she worked with the Central Social Welfare Board and the Planning Commission.
- Born 15 July 1909, Rajahmundry.
- At 12, left school to protest English as the medium of teaching.
- Joined the Salt Satyagraha in Madras (1930); jailed in 1930 and 1932.
- Elected from Madras; member of the Rules of Procedure committee.
- *Mithu says: From protester to lawmaker!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net*

**10. Hasrat Mohani** — हसरत मोहानी

Hasrat Mohani was an Urdu poet and a freedom fighter. He coined "Inquilab Zindabad". In 1921, long before 1930, he asked the Congress to demand complete independence. He was elected to the Assembly from the United Provinces.
- Born 1875, Mohan village, Unnao district.
- Coined the slogan “Inquilab Zindabad”.
- In 1921 he moved a Congress resolution calling for complete independence.
- A poet. Elected from the United Provinces on a Muslim League ticket.
- *Mithu says: Inquilab Zindabad: long live the revolution!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net*

**11. J.J.M. Nichols Roy** — जे.जे.एम. निकोल्स रॉय

Nichols Roy was a Khasi leader from the hills of today’s Meghalaya. He worked so that tribal areas of the North-East could run many of their own affairs through district councils. This is in the Sixth Schedule of the Constitution.
- Born 12 June 1884, Shella, in present-day Meghalaya. From the Khasi community.
- Elected from Assam.
- Member of the sub-committee on the North-East tribal areas.
- Called the “pioneer behind the Sixth Schedule”: self-governing district councils for tribal areas.
- *Mithu says: Sixth Schedule = self-rule for hill areas!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net*

### Chapter 3 The Founding Mothers संविधान की संस्थापक माताएँ

**12. Founding Fathers? And Mothers!** — संस्थापक माताएँ

We often hear "founding fathers". Achyut Chetan’s book shows that women members also helped write the Constitution, but their names were forgotten for many years. The book tells their story.
- Book: Founding Mothers of the Indian Republic, by Achyut Chetan (2022).
- It questions the phrase “founding fathers”.
- “When the Constituent Assembly was finally formed, 15 of its members were women.”
- *Mithu says: Mothers of the Constitution too!* · *Source: cambridge.org*

**13. Two lists of women** — महिलाओं की दो सूचियाँ

Membership changed over time, for example after Partition. Chetan’s list from the time the Assembly was formed includes two women who are not on the usual list of 15. Renuka Ray and Annie Mascarene joined later. In a quiz, "15 women" is the standard answer, but read the question carefully.
- Chetan’s list includes Begum Jahanara Shahnawaz and Begum Shaista Suhrawardy Ikramullah.
- Renuka Ray joined after Bengal’s seats went to East Pakistan (July 1947).
- Annie Mascarene joined in December 1948.
- The usual quiz answer is still: 15 women members.
- *Mithu says: Read the question carefully!* · *Source: cambridge.org; constitutionofindia.net*

**14. How the women worked** — महिलाओं ने कैसे काम किया

According to a review of Chetan’s book, the women members did not always make long speeches. They often sent written proposals, supported each other’s ideas, and wrote letters to influential members.
- They often gave their ideas in writing.
- They worked as a team: Amrit Kaur put forward ideas on Hansa Mehta’s behalf while Mehta was at the UN.
- They wrote letters to powerful male members to get their ideas heard.
- *Mithu says: Teamwork and letters!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net*

**15. Article 15(3)** — अनुच्छेद 15(3)

Article 15 says the State must not discriminate. Clause 3 adds that the State can still make special provisions for women and children. The review of Chetan’s book says the women members’ demands developed into this clause.
- Article 15(1): the State shall not discriminate against any citizen on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth.
- Article 15(3): “Nothing in this article shall prevent the State from making any special provision for women and children.”
- Adopted 29 November 1948 (as Draft Article 9).
- *Mithu says: Equal, plus special help!* · *Source: constitutionofindia.net*

### Chapter 4 Think like a quizmaster क्विज़ मास्टर की तरह सोचो

**16. Find the connection** — संबंध खोजो

Quizmasters love "connect" questions. Group the makers by where they were elected from, which committee they were on, and what cause they fought for.

## The 4 big leaders

### 1. Dr Rajendra Prasad · डॉ. राजेन्द्र प्रसाद
- Born: 3 December 1884, Siwan district, Bihar
- Died: 28 February 1963
- Education / profession: M.A. in Economics, University of Calcutta (1907); studied law at Calcutta Law College. / Lawyer at the Calcutta High Court, then the Patna High Court.
- Entry into the freedom struggle / public life: Joined the Indian National Congress in 1911. Gave up his law career in 1920 to join the independence movement.
- Milestones before 1947: 1917: joined Gandhi in the Champaran Satyagraha for indigo farmers. / President of the Congress three times. / Jailed in 1931 (Salt Satyagraha) and 1942 (Quit India).
- Known for: India’s first President, and the only President re-elected for a second term. Bharat Ratna.
- In the Constituent Assembly: Bihar · Congress
- Contribution to the Constitution: Elected permanent Chairman (later called President) of the Constituent Assembly on 11 December 1946. / Chaired four committees: Rules of Procedure, Steering, Finance and Staff, and the National Flag committee.
- After independence: Elected President of India on 24 January 1950. Retired from public life in 1962.
- Quiz clue: Coined the slogan “Grow More Food” as Food and Agriculture Minister in the Interim Government (1946).

### 2. Jawaharlal Nehru · जवाहरलाल नेहरू
- Born: 14 November 1889, Allahabad
- Died: 27 May 1964, New Delhi
- Education / profession: Harrow School and Trinity College, Cambridge; trained in law at the Inner Temple, London. / Advocate of the Allahabad High Court from 1912.
- Entry into the freedom struggle / public life: Began working for the Congress after returning from London in 1912.
- Milestones before 1947: 1920: led the Non-Cooperation and Kisan movements in the United Provinces. / 1928: protested against the Simon Commission. / December 1929: presided over the Lahore Congress, which chose Purna Swaraj (complete independence). / 1942: Quit India Movement. / Close to 9 years in British prisons in all.
- Known for: India’s first Prime Minister (1947–1964). His birthday is Children’s Day. Books: The Discovery of India, Glimpses of World History.
- In the Constituent Assembly: United Provinces · Congress
- Contribution to the Constitution: 13 December 1946: moved the Objectives Resolution; the Preamble is based on it. / Chaired the Union Powers Committee and the Union Constitution Committee. / 14 August 1947: “Tryst with destiny” speech.
- After independence: Prime Minister until his death in 1964.
- Quiz clue: Objectives Resolution + “Tryst with destiny”.

### 3. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel · सरदार वल्लभभाई पटेल
- Born: 31 October 1875, Nadiad, Gujarat
- Died: 15 December 1950, Bombay
- Education / profession: Nadiad High School; Middle Temple, London. / Barrister.
- Entry into the freedom struggle / public life: 1920: elected President of the new Gujarat Pradesh Congress Committee.
- Milestones before 1947: 1923: led the Satyagraha in Nagpur while Gandhi was in prison. / 1928: led the Bardoli Satyagraha, a farmers’ protest against higher taxes. / 1931: Congress President at the Karachi session (Karachi Resolution). / Non-Cooperation, Satyagraha and Quit India; arrested several times.
- Known for: “Iron Man of India”: brought the 565 princely states into the Union of India.
- In the Constituent Assembly: Bombay · Congress
- Contribution to the Constitution: Chairman, Provincial Constitution Committee. / Chairman, Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Minorities and Tribal and Excluded Areas. / 26 May 1949: welcomed that minority representatives had given up separate electorates.
- After independence: India’s first Deputy Prime Minister and Home Minister; Minister for States. Died in December 1950.
- Quiz clue: 565 princely states.

### 4. Dr B.R. Ambedkar · डॉ. भीमराव अम्बेडकर
- Born: 14 April 1891, Mhow Army Cantonment
- Died: 6 December 1956, Delhi
- Education / profession: B.A., Elphinstone College, Bombay; M.A., Columbia University; doctorate, University of London (1923); second doctorate, Columbia (1927). / Joined the Accountant-General’s Office in Baroda (1918) but left within 11 days: he could not find a place to stay because of his caste.
- Entry into the freedom struggle / public life: 1919: presented safeguards for the “untouchables” to the Southborough Committee.
- Milestones before 1947: 1924: founded the Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha. Motto: “Educate, Agitate, Organise”. / 20 March 1927: Mahad Satyagraha, Dalits drank water from the public Chavdar tank. / 1932: Poona Pact. / 1936: founded the Independent Labour Party; 1942: All-India Scheduled Castes Federation.
- Known for: Chairman of the Drafting Committee; called the “Father of the Indian Constitution”. Bharat Ratna (1991).
- In the Constituent Assembly: First Bengal, then Bombay
- Contribution to the Constitution: Chairman of the Drafting Committee (appointed 29 August 1947); it sat for 141 days. / 4 November 1948: introduced the Draft Constitution. / 9 December 1948: called Article 32 “the very soul of the Constitution and the very heart of it”.
- After independence: India’s first Law Minister (1947); resigned in 1951 after the Hindu Code Bill was withdrawn. Embraced Buddhism in 1956.
- Quiz clue: Mahad, 1927.

## More of the 10 Makers

### 5. Jaipal Singh Munda · जयपाल सिंह मुंडा

# Known source errors and quiz traps

- Nehru: constitutionofindia.net page says he died in 1967 and was PM 1947–1962 — both wrong. Profile uses 27 May 1964 (Britannica).
- Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar: his page calls him chairman of the Advisory Committee; the committee report is signed by Patel. Profile keeps Patel.
- Nine fields across the 24 profiles say 'Not stated in the source' (e.g. Purnima Banerji's exact dates, Kamla Chaudhri's Assembly contributions, Annie Mascarene's entry into the struggle).
- Leela Roy (resigned against Partition) and Malati Choudhury (resigned to work with Gandhi) are still on the standard list of 15 — quiz trap.
- Chetan's list of women at formation includes Begum Jahanara Shahnawaz and Begum Shaista Suhrawardy Ikramullah; Renuka Ray and Annie Mascarene joined later. Standard answer remains 15.
- Hindi spellings of names vary (अम्बेडकर / आंबेडकर).
