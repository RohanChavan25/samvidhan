# M1 · The road to a Constitution · संविधान की ओर  
*1928 – 1946*

### 💡 What is a Constituent Assembly?
A group of elected people who write a constitution. India’s first met on 9 December 1946.

*Sources:* constitutionofindia.net: Congress Working Committee, June 1934 · *Replaces:* m3-02

### 1928 · The Nehru Report 🆕
An early Indian draft constitution, credited to Motilal Nehru. It said “Men and women shall have equal rights as citizens” and listed fundamental rights.

*People:* Motilal Nehru
*Sources:* constitutionofindia.net: Nehru Report (Motilal Nehru, 1928)  
*Replaces:* audit · *Images:* new-e1928

### 26 January 1930 · Purna Swaraj declared
Indians publicly declared complete independence (Purna Swaraj) as their goal. The Constitution later came into force on 26 January 1950 to honour this day.

> **Story (m3-02):** On 26 January 1930, Indians publicly declared that their goal was Purna Swaraj, complete independence. In June 1934 the Congress officially demanded a Constituent Assembly: an elected body that would write India’s own constitution.  
> *Mithu: Constituent Assembly = the Constitution-writing team!*

*People:* Jawaharlal Nehru
*Sources:* constitutionofindia.net: Declaration of Purna Swaraj

### June 1934 · First demand for a Constituent Assembly
The Congress officially demanded a Constituent Assembly: a group of elected people who would write India’s own constitution (Congress Working Committee, 16–17 June 1934).
*Sources:* constitutionofindia.net: Congress Working Committee, June 1934  
*Replaces:* T19-2, m3-02, T2 · *Images:* T02

### 1935 · Government of India Act
Passed by the British Parliament in London. Laws for India were still made far away, and Indians wanted to write their own rules.

> **Story (m3-01):** For a long time, the laws that governed India were made under British rule. The Government of India Act 1935, for example, was passed by the British Parliament in London. Indians wanted to decide their own rules.  
> *Mithu: Our country, our rulebook!*
*Sources:* constitutionofindia.net: Congress Working Committee, June 1934  
*Replaces:* T19-3, m3-01, T3 · *Images:* m3-01, T03

### 16 May 1946 · Cabinet Mission Plan
The British Cabinet Mission announced a plan for a Constituent Assembly: 389 seats in all, of which the princely states could get up to 93.

> **Story (m3-03):** On 16 May 1946 the British Cabinet Mission announced a plan for the Constituent Assembly. In July 1946 the members were chosen. Ordinary voters did not vote directly; members of the provincial assemblies chose them. There were 389 seats, and the princely states could get up to 93 of them.  
> *Mithu: Plan first, then elections!*
*Sources:* constitutionofindia.net: Cabinet Mission Plan, 16 May 1946; Lok Sabha: Constituent Assembly of India  
*Replaces:* T19-4, C01-1, m3-03, T4 · *Images:* m3-03, T04

### July 1946 · Members chosen
Members of the provincial assemblies elected the members of the Constituent Assembly. Ordinary voters did not vote directly (an indirect election).
*Sources:* Lok Sabha: Constituent Assembly of India

### 💡 Where was Gandhi?
Mahatma Gandhi was not a member of the Constituent Assembly. But his ideas are in the Constitution: Article 17 abolishes untouchability, and Article 40 asks the State to organise village panchayats.

### The midnight session, minute by minute
- **11:00 p.m., 14 Aug 1947** — The fifth session begins in Constitution Hall, New Delhi. Sucheta Kripalani sings the first verse of Vande Mataram.
- **Before midnight** — Jawaharlal Nehru: “Long years ago we made a tryst with destiny…”
- **As the clock struck twelve** — Members take the pledge of service to India.
- **After midnight** — Hansa Mehta presents the National Flag “on behalf of the women of India”.
- **Close of session** — Sucheta Kripalani sings the first lines of Sare Jahan Se Achcha and the first verse of Jana Gana Mana.

### 💡 Adopt, enact, come into force
Adopt (अंगीकृत करना), 26 Nov 1949: the Assembly formally accepts the final text. Enact (अधिनियमित करना), 26 Nov 1949: the accepted text is made law. Come into force (लागू होना), 26 Jan 1950: the law starts working; Article 394 calls it “the commencement”. Like in school: printed in November, used from the new term.

*Sources:* Constitution of India, official text (1 May 2024); Hindi terms: Hindi Wikipedia, LiveLaw Hindi ⚠secondary · *Replaces:* C03, P13

### 💡 Heroes behind the scenes
B.N. Rau, the Constitutional Adviser, prepared the first draft. S.N. Mukherjee, the Chief Draftsman, turned ideas into clear legal language. Prem Behari Narain Raizada handwrote the English original, and Nandalal Bose and artists of Santiniketan decorated it. Ambedkar thanked Rau and Mukherjee on 25 November 1949.

# M4 · The Preamble · उद्देशिका  
*Word by word*

### P01 · The Preamble: India’s promise — उद्देशिका: भारत का वादा
- The Preamble: India's promise
- उद्देशिका: भारत का वादा
- (Uddeshika: Bharat ka vaada)
- The opening paragraph of the Constitution. In a few lines it answers four questions.
- WHO made it?
- We, the People of India
- हम, भारत के लोग
- WHAT is India?
- Sovereign · Socialist · Secular · Democratic · Republic
- WHAT does India promise?
- Justice · Liberty · Equality · Fraternity
- न्याय · स्वतंत्रता · समता · बन्धुता
- WHEN was it adopted?
- 26 November 1949
- Like in school: The first page of your school diary says what the school stands for. The rest of the diary is the rules.
*Source:* Constitution of India (official text, 1 May 2024)

### P02 · The full text — उद्देशिका का पूरा पाठ
- The full text
- उद्देशिका का पूरा पाठ
- (Uddeshika ka poora paath)
- WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC and to secure to all its citizens:
- JUSTICE, social, economic and political;
- LIBERTY of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship;
- EQUALITY of status and of opportunity;
- and to promote among them all
- FRATERNITY assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation;
- IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION.
- Highlighted words were added by the 42nd Amendment (in force 3 January 1977).
*Source:* Constitution of India (official text, 1 May 2024); 42nd Amendment Act, 1976

### P03 · We, the People of India — हम, भारत के लोग
- WHO MADE IT
- We, the People of India
- हम, भारत के लोग
- (Hum, Bharat ke log)
- Meaning
- The Constitution’s authority comes from the people of India, not from a king or a foreign ruler.
- Example
- Every citizen aged 18 or above has the right to vote, unless disqualified by law.
- Like in school
- The class rules are written by the whole class, not handed down by one person.
- Promise kept in
- Article 326: elections on the basis of adult suffrage
*Source:* Constitution of India (official text, 1 May 2024), Article 326

### P04 · Sovereign — संपूर्ण प्रभुत्व-संपन्न
- WHAT INDIA IS
- Sovereign
- संपूर्ण प्रभुत्व-संपन्न
- (Sampoorn prabhutva-sampann)
- Meaning
- India makes its own decisions. No outside power controls its laws or government.
- Example
- India’s Parliament, not any foreign government, decides India’s laws.
- Like in school
- Your school makes its own rules; another school cannot make rules for it.
- Promise kept in
- The whole Constitution: all power comes from it
*Source:* Constitution of India (official text, 1 May 2024)

### P05 · Socialist — समाजवादी
- WHAT INDIA IS
- ADDED IN 1976
- Socialist
- समाजवादी
- (Samajwadi)
- Meaning
- The State works for the economic and social upliftment of all people. In 2024 the Supreme Court said this does not stop private business.
- Example
- Free and compulsory education for every child aged 6 to 14 (Article 21A).
- Like in school
- The school makes sure every child has books, not only those who can pay.
- Promise kept in
- Article 38: the State shall promote the welfare of the people
*Source:* Constitution of India; 42nd Amendment Act, 1976; Dr Balram Singh v. Union of India, 25 Nov 2024

### P06 · Secular — पंथनिरपेक्ष
- WHAT INDIA IS
- ADDED IN 1976
- Secular
- पंथनिरपेक्ष
- (Panthnirpeksh)
- Meaning
- The State has no religion of its own. It “neither supports any religion nor penalizes the profession and practice of any faith” (Supreme Court, 2024).
- Example
- Every person may follow, practise and spread their religion, subject to public order, morality and health.
- Like in school
- No student is treated better or worse because of their religion.
- Promise kept in
- Article 25 (freedom of religion) and Article 15 (no discrimination on grounds of religion)
*Source:* Constitution of India, Articles 15 and 25; Dr Balram Singh v. Union of India, 25 Nov 2024

### P07 · Democratic — लोकतंत्रात्मक
- WHAT INDIA IS
- Democratic
- लोकतंत्रात्मक
- (Loktantratmak)
- Meaning
- The people choose their government through elections and can change it at the next election.
- Example
- Voting for an MLA, MP or corporator.
- Like in school
- The class monitor is chosen by the students’ votes.
- Promise kept in
- Article 326: adult suffrage
*Source:* Constitution of India (official text, 1 May 2024), Article 326

### P08 · Republic — गणराज्य
- WHAT INDIA IS
- Republic
- गणराज्य
- (Ganrajya)
- Meaning
- The head of the country is elected, not born into the job. There is no king or queen.
- Example
- The President is elected by the elected members of Parliament and of the State Legislative Assemblies.
- Like in school
- The head girl or head boy is chosen by election, not because a parent held the post.
- Promise kept in
- Article 54: election of the President
*Source:* Constitution of India (official text, 1 May 2024), Article 54

### P09 · Justice — न्याय
- WHAT INDIA PROMISES
- Justice
- न्याय
- (Nyay)
- Meaning
- Fairness of three kinds: social (no one treated as lower by birth), economic (a fair chance to earn and live) and political (an equal say in elections).
- In Hindi
- सामाजिक, आर्थिक और राजनीतिक न्याय
- Example
- Untouchability is abolished, and practising it in any form is forbidden.
- Like in school
- Every child gets the same seat, the same water tap and the same chance to answer.
- Promise kept in
- Articles 15 and 17 (social) · Article 38 (economic) · Article 326 (political)
*Source:* Constitution of India, Articles 15, 17, 38, 326; Hindi Preamble text

### P10 · Liberty — स्वतंत्रता
- WHAT INDIA PROMISES
- Liberty
- स्वतंत्रता
- (Swatantrata)
- Thought
- Expression
- Belief
- Faith
- Worship
- Meaning
- Freedom of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship.
- In Hindi
- विचार, अभिव्यक्ति, विश्वास, धर्म और उपासना की स्वतंत्रता
- Example
- Writing to a newspaper about a bad road. The law can still set reasonable limits, for example for public order.
- Like in school
- You may give your opinion in class, but you may not insult or threaten others.
- Promise kept in
- Article 19(1)(a), with limits under Article 19(2) · Article 25 (religion)
*Source:* Constitution of India, Articles 19 and 25; Hindi Preamble text

### P11 · Equality — समता
- WHAT INDIA PROMISES
- Equality
- समता
- (Samata)
- Equality of status
- Equality of opportunity
- Meaning
- Equality of status (प्रतिष्ठा) and of opportunity (अवसर).
- Why it matters
- The same law applies to a minister and a vegetable seller, and everyone gets a fair chance.
- Example
- Government jobs are open to all citizens, and the State cannot give people titles (only military or academic distinctions are allowed).
- Like in school
- Same exam, same rules and same marking for every student.
- Promise kept in
- Article 14 · Article 16 · Article 18
*Source:* Constitution of India, Articles 14, 16, 18; Hindi Preamble text

### P12 · Fraternity — बन्धुता
- WHAT INDIA PROMISES
- Fraternity
- बन्धुता
- (Bandhuta)
- Meaning
- Brotherhood and sisterhood among all Indians, assuring the dignity of every person and the unity and integrity of the nation.
- In Hindi
- व्यक्ति की गरिमा और राष्ट्र की एकता और अखण्डता
- Did you know?
- The word “integrity” was added in 1976. “Fraternity” was added by the Drafting Committee; it was not in the Objectives Resolution.
- Like in school
- Classmates help each other instead of forming groups that leave others out.
- Promise kept in
- Article 51A(e): a Fundamental Duty to promote harmony and common brotherhood
*Source:* Constitution of India, Article 51A; 42nd Amendment Act, 1976; Ambedkar’s note with the Draft Constitution, 1948

### P13 · Adopt, enact and give to ourselves — अंगीकृत, अधिनियमित और आत्मार्पित
- WHEN
- Adopt, enact and give to ourselves
- अंगीकृत, अधिनियमित और आत्मार्पित
- (Angeekrit, adhiniyamit aur aatmarpit)
- 26 November 1949
- Adopt
- Accept the final text.
- Enact
- Make it law.
- Give to ourselves
- The people own it. This is why 26 November is Constitution Day.
- Like in school
- The class signs its own rulebook, so everyone owns it.
- Quiz trap: the date inside the Preamble is 26 Nov 1949, not 26 Jan 1950.
*Source:* Constitution of India, Preamble (official text, 1 May 2024); Hindi Preamble text

### P14 · Why the promises go together — स्वतंत्रता · समता · बन्धुता
- Why the promises go together
- स्वतंत्रता · समता · बन्धुता
- (Swatantrata · Samata · Bandhuta)
- Liberty
- स्वतंत्रता
- Equality
- समता
- Fraternity
- बन्धुता
- Separate any one, and democracy fails
- These principles of liberty, equality and fraternity form a union of trinity in the sense that to divorce one from the other is to defeat the very purpose of democracy.
- — Dr B.R. Ambedkar, Constituent Assembly, 25 November 1949
- Freedom without equality lets the strong push the weak around.
- Equality without freedom means nobody can choose.
- Without fraternity, people will not respect each other's freedom or equality.
*Source:* Dr B.R. Ambedkar, Constituent Assembly, 25 Nov 1949

### P15 · Timeline 1: making the Preamble — उद्देशिका कैसे बनी
- Timeline 1: making the Preamble
- उद्देशिका कैसे बनी
- (Uddeshika kaise bani)
- 13 Dec 1946

- 21 Feb 1948

- 26 Nov 1949
- Preamble adopted with the Constitution. This date is written in it.
- 26 Jan 1950
- Comes into force: India is a “SOVEREIGN DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC”.
- Step in the making
- Big date

### 💡 Two lists of women
Membership changed over time, for example after Partition. Achyut Chetan’s list from when the Assembly was formed includes Begum Jahanara Shahnawaz and Begum Shaista Suhrawardy Ikramullah, who are not on the usual list of 15. Renuka Ray joined after Bengal’s seats went to East Pakistan (July 1947); Annie Mascarene joined in December 1948. In a quiz, “15 women” is the standard answer, but read the question carefully.

*Sources:* Achyut Chetan, Founding Mothers of the Indian Republic (Cambridge University Press, 2022) and chapter 'In Search of the Missing Mothers' ⚠secondary; constitutionofindia.net: women members · *Replaces:* m4-13

### 💡 How the women worked
According to a review of Chetan’s book, the women members did not always make long speeches. They often gave their ideas in writing, supported each other (Amrit Kaur put forward ideas on Hansa Mehta’s behalf while Mehta was at the UN), and wrote letters to influential members.

*Sources:* Review of Chetan by Aishwarya Birla (PACT), on constitutionofindia.net, 15 Nov 2023 ⚠secondary · *Replaces:* m4-14

### 💡 Article 15(3)
Article 15(1): the State shall not discriminate against any citizen on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth. Article 15(3): “Nothing in this article shall prevent the State from making any special provision for women and children.” Adopted 29 November 1948 as Draft Article 9. A review of Chetan’s book says the women members’ demands developed into this clause.

*Sources:* constitutionofindia.net: Article 15 (Draft Article 9), adopted 29 Nov 1948; Review of Chetan by Aishwarya Birla (PACT), on constitutionofindia.net, 15 Nov 2023 ⚠secondary · *Replaces:* m4-15

## Quiz traps
- The Preamble’s date is 26 November 1949, not 26 January 1950.
- Adopted and enacted on the same day (26 Nov 1949); 26 Jan 1950 is when it came into force.
- “15 women” is the standard answer, but Chetan’s formation-day list differs. Read the question.
- Leela Roy (resigned against Partition) and Malati Choudhury (resigned to work with Gandhi) are still on the standard list of 15.
- Gandhi was not a member of the Constituent Assembly.
- “Fraternity” was not in the Objectives Resolution; the Drafting Committee added it.
- The Preamble has been amended only once (42nd Amendment, 1976).
- There were two Vice-Presidents: H.C. Mookherjee and V.T. Krishnamachari (16 July 1947).
- B.N. Rau prepared the first draft; the Drafting Committee examined and revised it. “Chief architect” is a title, not a one-man job.
- On 14–15 August 1947 the flag was presented after midnight, i.e. on 15 August, in the sitting that began on 14 August.
- Hindi spellings of names vary (अम्बेडकर / आंबेडकर). Use your textbook’s if it differs.
